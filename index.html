MYTH“Sunscreen causes cancer”

No evidence supports this. The Skin Cancer Foundation reports that sunscreen helps prevent skin cancer, and ultraviolet light is the proven cause of most skin cancers.

Example result. Sources: Skin Cancer Foundation, Harvard Health

No tool? Check it yourself

Who said it? Look for a doctor, a hospital or a health agency, not only an influencer.

Do health authorities agree? Search the claim on WHO, CDC or NHS sites.

Is it trying to scare you? Words like “they hide this” are a warning sign.

Is someone selling something? A miracle product often explains the claim.

MythBuster

Evidence-based explanations backed by trusted health authorities to build stronger digital health literacy. (Placeholder: additional verified claims from the Research Team will be added here.)

Sunscreen causes cancer.
MYTH

The Skin Cancer Foundation says there is no evidence that sunscreen causes cancer and that sunscreen has been shown to prevent it. A Harvard dermatologist explains that studies linking sunscreen users to more skin cancer reflect heavy sun exposure, not the sunscreen.

Skin Cancer FoundationHarvard Health
Detox teas flush toxins out of your body.
NOT SUPPORTED

Your liver and kidneys do this work every day. A spokesperson for the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics told PolitiFact there is no strong evidence that detox diets or cleanses improve health in healthy adults.

PBS NewsHour with PolitiFact
Antibiotics help you get over a cold or the flu.
MYTH

The CDC explains that antibiotics treat bacterial infections, not viruses. For a cold or flu they will not make you feel better, and using them when they are not needed adds to antibiotic resistance.

CDC, Antibiotic Do’s and Don’ts
Staying hydrated helps your kidneys.
TRUE

Kidneys filter the blood to make urine, and staying hydrated helps them clear waste. People with kidney or liver disease should follow their doctor’s advice on fluids.

PBS NewsHour with PolitiFact
